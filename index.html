The problem

Retail fulfillment is still human work

Stores are unstructured, occupied, and unforgiving. Stocking, picking, and restocking demand contact with the world — force, touch, judgment in crowded aisles — that today's robots cannot yet do alone. Labor is scarce. The work does not wait.

  • Unstructured aisles Every SKU, shelf, and shopper is different. There is no clean factory cell — only messy, living retail floors.
  • Contact is the bottleneck Vision alone is not enough. Physical AGI needs force, tactile sense, and proprioception learned from real work.
  • Labor that will not scale Fulfillment demand grows; the workforce to meet it does not. Automation has to earn trust on the job, not in the lab.

Our hypothesis: the path to physical AGI runs through automating retail fulfillment — training foundation models on the contact of real American work.
